About Me —

My introduction to design didn't begin with pixels—it began with pillars. As a teenager, I watched my brother design an international convention center, and something clicked (or maybe short-circuited). The sheer thoughtfulness behind every decision—the scale, the process, the idea that spaces can shape how people feel—left me both inspired and slightly overwhelmed. Design wasn't just about things looking good. It was about making things work—for people, for moments, for the future.

Around the same time, I discovered another kind of design—on a computer screen. I started playing with HTML and CSS on my brother's PC. This was 2007, when web design was 90% tables and 10% prayer. I had no clue what I was doing, but I was hooked. The internet became my sandbox—and my silent mentor.

I went on to study Computer Science at Guru Nanak Dev University and graduated in 2013. Tried (and failed) to get into NID and IDC—twice, for good measure. But failure has a strange way of pointing you to Plan B, which in my case, turned out to be the better plan. I kept learning, designing, breaking things, fixing them, reading blogs, and staying up way too late experimenting. No curriculum, no grades—just curiosity, caffeine, and CSS.

Today, I'm a self-taught Product Designer with a strong focus on user research, product strategy, and visual design. I see design as a multidisciplinary craft where empathy meets logic, and creativity shakes hands with constraint. It's less about pushing pixels, more about solving problems and asking the right questions (and occasionally, the wrong ones too—just to be sure).

Outside of work, I read a lot (and finish some), meditate inconsistently, admire good typography like it's fine art, and chase thoughts around the internet like a digital squirrel. I'm deeply curious about art, spirituality, stoicism, karma yoga, and what Iceland looks like in winter. My playlists swing between Sigur Rós, Ólafur Arnalds, and Krishna Das—basically, if it makes you cry in a snowstorm, I'm in.

Still learning. Still tinkering. Still asking why not.
